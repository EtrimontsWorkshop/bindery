## [0.4.1]

### Added

- **Drawings made of vector paths can be offered (module setting).** A cartoon figure drawn as vector paths (the blue vault dwellers beside the attribute descriptions of a rulebook), a diagram, a logo has no raster in the PDF, so nothing of it was ever offered; only wide rectangular fills (maps) were looked for. With the new module setting **Import vector drawings** on, the filled paths that are not rectangles are collected, and a cluster of at least 15 of them that lie within 3 pt of one another, fill their box and make a figure of 1%-50% of the page (not a table or a banner: at most 5 times longer than wide and at most 78% of the page's width) is rendered and offered as an undecided picture, unchecked. A drawing that a picture offered on the page already covers, one that is found at the same place on three pages or more (a running ornament) and one whose render is white over 80% of its pixels (a header plate on an empty page) are left out. The text of the page that runs past a drawing is painted over in the render wherever no shape of the drawing lies, with the color of the page around it; a scrap of text can remain where a wide shape lies over it. On a rulebook with a hundred figures of this kind all of them come out; the other books of a set of twenty-two add a handful of diagrams, logos and tables. With the setting off (the default) the PDF is analysed as before.
- **An illustration split in two by the gutter of a spread is joined.** One picture that runs across two facing pages (a painter with a book in his hand on the left page and the book on the right one, a double-page panorama, a dragon across the fold) was imported as two halves. The joining that was done for the halves of a map (big translucent panels) now also applies to the halves of an illustration: a picture that reaches the right edge of its page and one that starts at the left edge of the next page, with the same top and bottom, are joined when their pixels go on across the seam (something is drawn on both sides in the same rows, and the colors change across it no more than they change inside the pictures). Two unrelated pictures that merely meet at the gutter (two portraits, a board and a sheet of paper) are kept apart. The right half need not be offered by itself. Pairs that this cannot tell apart, such as two full-page scans of facing pages, may still be joined.

### Changed

- **Polish letters in the window title are drawn in the same typeface.** The display font of the window title and of the empty-state heading (Caprasimo) has no ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ś, ź, ż, so the browser drew them in a system serif font, which looked wrong inside a word. Those two texts now use the bold weight of Figtree, which the module already ships and which covers them; the unused Caprasimo files and license text were removed.

### Fixed

- **A portrait and the card of text laid over it are imported separately.** A character's portrait with a parchment holding the stat block over its lower part was imported as one picture (the portrait, the parchment and the paragraph of the page between them). A unit that holds a card of text (at least 30% of its box covered by the page's text) and a picture of at least 10% of the page that the card covers only in part is now split into the picture and the card. A picture that lies on the card (a photo on a newspaper clipping, a signature on a sheet) leaves the card whole.
- **A card of text is imported with the ornaments of its frame that stick out of it.** A box of rules on parchment has a title plaque across its top edge and a flourish across its bottom edge; the render of the card was cut exactly at the card's box, so the title ("MAGIA") and the flourish came out cut in half. The region of a card of text now takes in small decorations that straddle its top or bottom edge (at least 30% inside the card, within 50 pt of its edge and 25 pt of its sides), except those that come within 30 pt of the edge of the page (running headers and footers).
- **Chapter openers with an illustration under a title banner are imported alike.** The illustration of one opener page came out alone (from its own pixels) and that of the next with the frame, the banner and its title, depending on whether the three masked pieces of the banner (a left cap, a plaque, a right cap) each had 5% of the page or just under it: the pieces grow with the title. In a unit whose main picture fills half the page or more, masked pieces of 4.5% of the page now count as pictures of their own, so the unit is split and the illustration is taken alone. Units under a smaller picture (a photo with a note and tape) are judged as before.
- **A layer with next to no visible pixels is no longer offered as a picture.** The glow of a flaming sword painted as a separate layer over an illustration, in a raster of the illustration's size, was offered as a second picture that showed a few specks on a transparent field. An image with fewer than 2% opaque pixels is now hidden, whatever its size (the two such layers of the books measured have 0.4% and 0.8%; the sparsest real picture has 3.1%).
- **An illustration is no longer imported together with the box of text beside it.** A picture of two figures with a box of rules laid over its corner was imported as a render of the page: the figures, the paragraph above them and the box. The box (a plain opaque panel with a lot of the page's text on it) counted as a second picture of the unit, which makes the whole unit be rendered. A member that is a card of the page's text no longer counts, and the picture is imported from its own pixels, without the text.
- **An illustration drawn in several pieces is imported as one picture.** The pieces of one illustration (sheets of paper, scrolls and pencils on a page) were imported separately when the largest of them reached past the edge of the page by a point: a picture that bleeds without anything speaking for it was never joined with its neighbors. A picture that reaches past the page by a point or two only now has its own reason (a flush picture) and is clustered like any other; the region of such a unit is the union of what is visible of its pieces (not of their rasters, whose transparent margins run into the margin of the page). A pale drawing that only touches the picture next to it is no longer taken for a paper vignette laid over it; a vignette must cover at least half of the picture it melts.
- **A picture painted twice is imported from its own pixels, without the frame the page draws around it.** The portrait of a character in a ring (the ring and its ornament are drawn by the page) came out framed on one page, where two pictures lay in the same place, and bare on another, where one did. A picture that lies exactly where the representative of its unit is (each covering 90% of the other) and is not cut out by a mask is that picture again and no longer makes the unit a composition to render.
- **A picture flush with the page edge and carrying a title or a caption is offered.** A picture that reaches past the edge of the page by a point or so (a column of pictures with caption banners, a chapter opener with its title across the picture, a figure cut out beside the text) was taken for a background with text on it when the title or banner covered 15% of the picture's center: the forest of a chapter opener, the figures of an adventure, the photos of a handbook were missing. A picture of less than 60% of the page that reaches past it at one or two edges (a corner) and by at most 2 pt is now taken for a background only when text covers 40% of its center (the title and banners measured cover up to 32%, sheets of paper with text 35% and more); in between it is left undecided, and the pixels of the picture decide. Print bleed (3 mm, three edges or more) is judged as before. Sheets of text that run flush with the edge may now be offered, unchecked, where they were hidden.
- **Dark, smooth illustrations are no longer hidden as smooth backgrounds.** A picture in red light, a portrait in a dim room or a smoky street is as smooth as a wash at both scales, because its gradients are small in absolute terms; the rule for smooth backgrounds hid a full-page illustration, three portraits and two rooms of a rulebook and a ship book. A smooth image with a mean luminance below 100 and a luminance spread of 15 or more is now kept; light sheets and washes and flat dark panels (spread below 15) are still hidden. The mean luminance is now measured for every extracted image.
- **A texture laid over a picture with a blend mode no longer hides the picture.** A map cut out by transparency (a cave on a dark page) was hidden because a page-sized concrete texture painted after it was taken for a picture covering it; the page composites that texture with the overlay blend mode, so the map shows through. Bindery now records the blend mode a picture is painted with (its own or its transparency group's), and a picture painted with a mode other than Normal no longer counts as covering what lies under it.
- **A page-sized dark scene is no longer hidden as a sheet of paper.** An undecided picture that fills the page and is smooth at the coarse scale was hidden as a sheet of paper, and the foggy half of a picture running across a spread went with it while the other half, slightly less smooth, was offered. Such a picture is now kept when its luminance spread is that of a picture (30 or more; the sheets of paper and washes measured are 20-22): night scenes, chapter openers, full-page art. A light page with a few dark shapes on it (a mean luminance of 200 or more) is still hidden.
- **An illustration painted twice is offered once.** An illustration at the foot of a page that the PDF paints once raw and once again cut out by a mask came out as two entries of different sizes (the raw one and a render of the cut-out copy), as on the pages of a rulebook with photos under a torn edge. A large masked picture that lies exactly where another picture of the page lies (each covering 90% of the other) is that picture again and is no longer offered; the raw one is.
- **Large illustrations cut out by a mask and running off the page are offered.** A picture that fills a third to seven tenths of the page, is cut out by a mask (rounded corners, an uneven edge) and reaches the edge of the page was taken for a scrap of the page's design and hidden: the spaceship illustrations at the foot of a page, pinned photos on a handout, concept art. They are now offered, unchecked. A picture of that kind that is mostly transparent (a blood splatter, a smudge) is still hidden, and a picture that fills the page is still taken for its background. Such a picture no longer hides the pictures under it.
- **A figure standing on a card of the layout is imported on its own.** A character standing in front of the panel with a stat block (the panel painted at 90%) was joined with the panel into one picture; only the figure that was cut off at the page edge came out alone. A big card of the layout and the figures on it are now separate pictures.
- **Every character card with a stat block is imported, not only some.** A plain panel with a stat block on it was imported as what the page shows only when the text covered 30% of it, and as the bare panel otherwise: of cards of one size, one to a page, some came out and the rest were left out. A smooth, opaque panel (up to 80% of the page, so a page background is not taken for one) with 15% or more text on it is now imported as what the page shows.
- **Small decorations on a page of many small pictures are no longer offered.** On a page laid out of small pictures and their masks (a crew list of portraits in a frame) a small undecided picture is part of the layout, as it was on pages crowded with tiny pictures.

## [0.4.0]

### Fixed

- **A picture that the page turns and shows through a rectangle is imported as the page shows it.** A painting placed on the page at a slight angle and cut by a plain rectangular window (an illustration in a text column) was imported from its raw pixels cut by the window turned the same way: a tilted frame with transparent corners. It is now sampled through the upright window at the raw resolution, so the picture comes out as the rectangle the page shows. It applies only when the window lies inside the picture; a tilted sheet or print lying on the page keeps being imported upright from its raw pixels.
- **Pictures of the same size at the same place on different pages are no longer taken for one picture.** The portraits of a set of ready characters (four cards, one portrait per page, the same size and position) were reduced to one: positionally matching pictures were treated as duplicates without comparing pixels, except for pictures filling half the page. The pixel comparison is now required from 5% of the page up; repeated small elements (frames, icons, headers) are still merged by position.
- **A dark panel of the page layout that only touches a picture no longer joins its unit.** The info panel beside a character's portrait was taken into the portrait's region, so one card came out as the portrait together with part of the table and the others as the portrait alone. A dark translucent panel is left out of a unit that has a solid picture.
- **A picture of 9.99% of the page is as much a picture as one of 10.01%.** Pictures of moderate size with no other evidence are offered as pictures from 9% of the page (it was 10%), so that equal cards no longer split into "picture" and "undecided" by a rounding difference.
- **Painted portraits are no longer hidden as smooth backgrounds.** The rule that hides a paper wash or a vignette also caught dark, softly painted portraits (as smooth as a wash at both scales), so most character portraits of a book were missing from the list. A smooth image whose luminance spread is as high as a figure's (30 or more; the washes and panels of the same book are 20-22) is now kept.
- **A map that runs across two pages is imported as one picture.** The two halves of such a map are big translucent panels (30%-40% opacity) painted over the page background; they were hidden as an overlay. A big translucent panel is now judged by its pixels: a colorful raster (a city map under a diagram) is a picture and is rendered with what is drawn on it, a dark bare texture is rendered when a drawing lies on it (checked as for the plans), and a panel with neither is still hidden. When such a picture on the right edge of one page meets one of the same height on the left edge of the next, the two renders are joined side by side into one image (listed on the first of the two pages). Light emblems, seals and illustrations painted translucent, and dark painted illustrations among text, are left alone.
- **A picture stored sideways and turned upright on the page is imported upright.** The raw picture of such a figure (a magazine cover placed turned by a quarter, tilted a little on the page) was imported as stored, lying on its side. Bindery now reads from the placement of the picture how many quarter turns the page applies and turns the imported picture the same way. Mirrored pictures and pictures with a diagonal turn are not affected.
- **Floor plans, maps and technical drawings on dark panels are imported as the picture the page shows.** The page paints such a drawing as vector lines over a translucent dark panel, so the picture found in the PDF was only the bare panel (a texture), and the plan itself was never offered. A translucent dark panel with hardly any text on it is now rendered together with what is drawn over it (checked by comparing a small render with the panel's own pixels) and offered as the picture, also when a sign sticks out of the panel (the sign comes with it); a panel with nothing drawn on it is still hidden.
- **The dark panels that the page lays behind its boxes and cards are no longer offered as pictures.** A rectangle of dark texture or gradient under a table, a sidebar or a weapon card came out as a bare texture, one for every box of the book. Recognized and hidden: a translucent panel (40%-65% opacity) with the page's text on it, a translucent dark texture whatever lies on it (a drawing, a table), and an opaque dark texture with text on it, as long as the classifier was not sure of it and the raster is opaque all over (a faint crest, a faded figure, a dark photograph and the lighter panels that are imported together with their text are not affected). A panel that merely touches a picture no longer drags it into one render: the picture is taken on its own. The picture that fills a character-sheet page (a page crowded with small pictures) and that nobody could judge is hidden as the backdrop of the sheet.
- **Re-importing a statblock no longer deletes Items you added by hand.** Every Actor and Item created by a profile import now records where it came from (the profile, the collection and a stable key of the entry). An update replaces only the Items that carry that mark, in collections whose text was found in the statblock; Items you added yourself, and Items of a collection that this statblock did not contain, stay. Actors and Items imported by an earlier version carry no mark, so the first update keeps them next to the new ones: the import reports how many there are. The new Items are created first and the old ones removed only after the Actor was updated, so a failure leaves the Actor as it was.
- **An update never silently picks the wrong Actor.** When several Actors share the name of an imported statblock, the one made from the same profile is chosen; if that is still not unique, the statblock is skipped with a warning instead of overwriting one of them.
- **A statblock with errors is no longer written.** An error found while the statblock was prepared (a mapped path that is gone, a value of the wrong type, a refused expression) now blocks the write and is listed in the report; before, the Actor was created incomplete and counted as imported. A changed type of a mapped field is found, and a changed schema of the template Actor is reported as a warning.
- **Statblocks that miss a required label are not imported by default.** They are still listed for diagnosis, unticked, with the missing labels shown.
- **A label is found even when the PDF splits its line differently.** Labels are matched on the reconstructed line, not on a single piece of text, and a value ends where the next known label of the profile starts. A label inside one piece of text ("Name: Goblin  HP: 7") now yields the value after it and no more.
- **A collection can end where its section ends.** The split rule has a new optional "section end" (a heading such as the next section's), so a section no longer swallows the sections that follow it. A statblock in which the section heading was not found leaves the Items on the Actor alone instead of treating the collection as empty.
- **Profile expressions are bounded.** Every pattern a profile uses is compiled in one place, with a length limit, a refusal of patterns whose shape can backtrack without bound (nested repetition, repeated alternatives), and a limit on the text it runs on. A profile with such a pattern fails validation and the pattern is refused at run time with a diagnostic. This reduces the risk of a profile freezing the browser tab; it does not run expressions in isolation, so a pattern that is slow in another way is still slow.

### Added

- **"Rotate left" and "Rotate right" in the image editor.** The window that opens from a thumbnail (preview and erase) turns the whole picture by a quarter turn. Each turn is one undo step (Ctrl+Z), "Restore original" turns the picture back before restoring its pixels, and the saved image takes the new size.
- **Arrow keys move through the images in the review.** With an image highlighted, ↓ and ↑ move the highlight to the next and the previous image of the list shown (switching the page preview when the image is on another page). Inside a field or a drop-down list the keys keep their usual meaning.
- **A "Sort images by page" setting.** In the module settings, next to "Add a white background to images". Off (the default) keeps the review list as it was: the images Bindery is sure about by page, then the undecided ones from the largest to the smallest. On, every image is listed in the order of the book, page by page and on a page from the top to the bottom, left to right.
- **The statblock engine for authoring modules gains `prepareStatblock`, `normalizeRectToBlock` and `validateProfileAgainstSchema`.** `prepareStatblock` runs the whole preparation of one statblock (extraction, casting, building, every diagnostic, and whether it may be imported) and is what the review and the import both use, so what an authoring tool previews is what is imported. `normalizeRectToBlock` turns a rectangle on the page into the block-relative form that a region source reads. The contract version is unchanged.

## [0.3.5]

### Fixed

- **Strips of tape, stamps and slivers at the page edge are no longer offered.** A masked raster that is a narrow strip (aspect 5 or more) is the tape that holds a photo to the page, whatever the size of its tilted box; a masked raster at up to 50% opacity and aspect 3 or more is a stamp laid over a table ("NOFORN"); a narrow sliver of a picture that bleeds off the page (the visible end of a document sheet, a piece of tape) is part of the page's design. A raster whose visible pixels are all white (a strip cut from a larger sheet by the page's clip) is hidden too.
- **Page backgrounds are no longer offered under the pictures that lie on them.** A picture that fills the page and could not be classified, with a picture judged to be content painted on it, is the background of that page (a textured section-opener sheet under a photo). The first page, the cover, is not affected.
- **Tables and forms drawn on a plain sheet are no longer offered as pictures.** A table or a sidebar whose raster is flat white (or flat dark) with the page's text over it, and a page-sized character-sheet form, were rendered as pictures of the page's own text. They are recognized by the look of the raw raster (luminance, its spread, saturation) and hidden. Rasters that are a solid fill under a mask, textured or coloured notes, and grey paper documents are not affected.
- **A paper note beside a text column is imported with its own writing and without the page text next to it.** A parchment note whose picture also holds a transparent margin (a shadow, a blood streak) was rendered together with that margin, so a strip of the neighboring text column came with it; a note whose text did not cover enough of the whole box was imported as the blank parchment. A note that is mostly visible and mostly covered by text is now judged and rendered by its visible part. This also works for notes that are mirrored on the page (the position of every picture now records whether it is mirrored or turned). Notes whose box holds only a torn margin, line art and faded figures are not touched.

## [0.3.4]

### Fixed

- **Tokens made from line art on a transparent ground are no longer blurred.** The token mask and the background removal smoothed their edges by blurring the picture's whole alpha channel, which also blurred sketches whose lines are drawn in alpha: thick, grey, smeared lines with blocks around them. Only the edge of the shape (or of the removed area) is smoothed now; opaque pictures come out as before.

## [0.3.3]

### Added

- **A global "Add a white background to images" setting.** In the module settings, next to "Import PDF…". When it is on, every image that has transparent areas gets a white background right after the PDF is analysed, so the review (thumbnails, editor, rotation) already shows it, and again when it is saved at import (which covers later edits). An image with no transparency is left untouched. It applies to every image, tokens and statblock portraits included; an image that you changed by hand (token panel, image editor) keeps what you did and gets no background at save.
- **"Fill background" in the image editor.** Puts the chosen color behind the whole picture, so that a sketch exported with a transparent background can be read. One undo step, like the erase tools.

### Fixed

- **Watermark crests are imported without the page text that lies over them.** A crest or emblem painted faintly (10%-20% opacity) behind a quotation or a text column used to be rendered together with that text. Such a translucent picture is now taken from the PDF as it is (the clean mark, unchecked "undecided" as before); the text is not part of it.
- **A paper splatter or stain that runs off the page is no longer imported as a piece of the page.** An undecided picture that bleeds off the page and has no text in its centre used to be rendered together with the body text that flows over its edge. It now comes out as the picture itself.
- **Photos pinned as strongly tilted prints no longer come with the white print border.** The more a print is tilted, the more of its mask is black, so photos tilted by more than about 10 degrees were still rendered as a rectangle of the page. Bindery now also recognizes a mask that is a tilted rectangle (by its shape, not only by how white it is) and takes the original photo. Masks with a different shape (a silhouette, an oval, a torn edge), and prints with a page text lying over them, are rendered as before.
- **A photo with a faint crest behind it is imported as the photo.** When a translucent crest laid under a pinned photo was larger than the photo, the crest decided how the unit was extracted and the photo came out with the page around it. The photo now represents the unit.
- **PDF documents and their workers are released after use.** Every analysis step opened the PDF in its own pdf.js worker and never closed it, so each import left several workers (each holding the whole file in memory) running until the page was reloaded, and repeated imports piled them up. Each step now releases its document as soon as it is done, also when it fails or is cancelled, and the bookmarks are read from the document that is already open. Measured in a browser on a short test file: after file inspection, statblock detection, image extraction, the review build and the page preview the old code left 10 workers running, the new code 0.
- **A picture cut from a larger sheet is taken as the page shows it.** Some layouts paint a figure from an image that holds several figures and let a clipping path hide the rest, so the original showed a piece of the neighbor that is not on the page. Bindery now reads the clipping paths and applies them to the original image: what the page hides becomes transparent and the result is cropped to what is visible. Many photos come out a few percent tighter for the same reason: the page shows less of them than the original holds.
- **Cut-out figures no longer come with the text around them.** A figure on a transparent ground is placed in a box that is larger than the figure, and the page text that runs around it lies in that box. It used to make Bindery render the page region together with the text. It now takes the figure itself, also when the text is a heading or a sidebar and when a colored backdrop shape stands behind the figure. A faded paper vignette laid over an illustration is not taken for the illustration, and the illustration is rendered without the strip of page that the vignette adds around it.
- **A picture painted a second time over itself at reduced opacity is no longer offered twice.** A tinted copy of the same artwork at 20%-60% opacity is part of that picture. The limit for "painted translucent" now also covers 0.6 as PDF stores it (0.600006).
- **Chapter-opener banners are no longer offered as images.** The halftone layer behind "Chapter N" is a small masked raster painted at 20% opacity (up to 6% of the page, 300 px on the long edge). Such a faint layer is now treated as decoration. Faded pictures painted at 30% and more (emblems, watermarks) are not affected.
- **Every map of a map set is offered, not just the first.** Full-page pictures of one size at the same place on every page (a booklet of maps, one to a page) were taken for repeats of the first one and merged with it. A picture that fills half the page or more is now a duplicate of another only when its pixels are identical.
- **The print hidden under another print is no longer offered.** A tilted print lies in the box of the whole rotated raster, so a print that the one on top covers almost completely (the white border and the shadow are all that shows) was not covered enough by the boxes. The check now uses the part that the mask lets through. This also hides four such back prints in a second rulebook, each a picture that is not on the page.
- **Torn paper scraps under a handout are no longer offered.** An opaque, light, flat and smooth picture that could not be classified (the scrap the crest or the note is drawn on) is hidden like other smooth backgrounds. Dark pictures that are as smooth are not affected.
- **Paper sheets under the text are no longer offered.** The torn sheets that a page lays its text on (80%-95% opaque because of their ragged edges, light and smooth) are hidden like other smooth backgrounds; the rule now looks at the visible pixels only.
- **Background removal in the token panel works on pictures with a white background.** A sketch on white is 83%-95% background, which the safeguard against a runaway flood fill (60% of the picture) took for a failed detection: the removal was cancelled and the white came back, also after a manual click on an extra area. The limit is now 98.5% when the four corners are white.
- **Black torn edges along the page are no longer offered.** A raster whose visible pixels are all black (the ragged black border of a page, a shadow) is hidden like other backgrounds.
- **Figures cut out against the page edge are offered again.** A masked figure that overhangs the page by a point or two was taken for a paper scrap or a stain that runs off the page and hidden. When its mask is a silhouette and the picture has real colors (not a splatter, smoke or a faded vignette), it is now offered.
- **Figures standing on colored panels are imported one by one, without the panel.** On a page where several figures stand on faint colored shapes, the shapes used to be offered as images of their own, or one of them stood for the whole group and the figure came out with its panel and the text around it. The faint shapes (20% opacity) are now left out of the unit and the figures are offered separately, as the figure alone.
- **A picture covered by a much larger one is hidden, a picture covered by a vignette is not.** Only a covering picture that is masked, page-sized or at least twice as large hides what is under it.

## [0.3.2]

### Fixed

- **Pictures next to page-edge pieces are no longer extracted with the page around them.** In some rulebooks a photo sits beside a coloured tab or a corner ornament that runs off the edge of the page. Bindery used to merge such a piece into the photo's unit, so the extracted image grew to the page edge and picked up the text and margins in between. These pieces now stay separate (offered as unchecked "undecided" images) and the photo is cut out on its own.
- **Pictures cut by page text now come out clean.** When a picture is partly covered by the page's own text (for example a portrait with a caption laid over its edge), Bindery used to render that page area and so copied the text into the image. It now takes the original image from the PDF instead, when that image has the same proportions as the place it is drawn in. The original has no frame or mask shape, and a picture whose original does not match what the page shows (a texture under a shape) is still rendered from the page as before.
- **Photos pinned to the page as tilted prints are extracted as the photo itself.** A photo with a tilted edge, a white print border or a drop shadow used to be cut out as a rectangle of the page, so it came with the page background, the margin strip and a neighbor's text. When the photo's mask lets most of it through and the original image has the proportions of the place it is drawn in, Bindery now takes the original image from the PDF (full resolution, no print border). A mask that hides a large part of the picture still gets the page render.
- **Paper scraps, stains and shadows that run off the page are no longer offered as images.** These pieces of the page's own design (a masked picture that bleeds off the page) used to show up as unchecked "undecided" images that were just a piece of the page with its text. They are now treated as decoration.
- **Taking the original photo now also works in the browser.** The PDF library hands decoded images over a few milliseconds after a page is read, and Bindery used to look for the mask of a photo before it had arrived, so in Foundry the photos pinned to the page as tilted prints were still rendered together with the page around them. Bindery now waits briefly for the images it needs.
- **The right image is taken from the PDF.** The library numbers images differently when pages without pictures are skipped, so an image could be mistaken for another one on the same page (a strip of paper or a mask instead of the photo). Bindery now reads the image's number from the page's own drawing list.
- **Pictures that overlap on a page are offered one by one.** Photos pinned to a page, stacked and overlapping, used to be merged into one unit and rendered as a whole block of the page (or, with the original photo taken, the others disappeared from the list). Now each photo or note that covers a real share of the page is its own image, and the tape, crests and paperclips on them stay with the picture they are on.
- **Text that lies on a picture stays with it.** A paper note or a newspaper handout is the blank paper in the PDF and the text is drawn by the page, so taking the original image gave only the background. Pictures with text over them (30% or more of their area) are rendered as the page shows them.
- **Tape, translucent ornaments and paper backgrounds are no longer offered as images.** Small elongated masked scraps (strips of adhesive tape), ornaments painted at 10%-50% opacity (watermarks, zodiac wheels) and page-sized sheets of paper are treated as decoration. They remain available through "Select & cut".
- **The hidden bottom of a stack of photos is no longer offered.** In a stack of pinned photos the one underneath is covered almost completely (75% or more) by the one on top and is not what the page shows. In some books the same filler picture lies under every stack, so it showed up many times.
- **Paperclips and other small cut-outs are no longer offered.** Small images that are mostly transparent (a paperclip, a pin) are treated as decoration.
- **Character sheets no longer fill the list with their label bars and boxes.** Images below 0.5% of the page that could not be classified are not offered, and on a page with a crowd of such images (a character sheet) its other small undecided parts are not offered either.

## [0.3.1]

### Fixed

- **PDF analysis no longer fails when something else on the page has frozen `Math`.** With some combinations of modules or browser extensions, analyzing a PDF failed with "Cannot define property sumPrecise, object is not extensible" because the PDF library tries to add a missing `Math.sumPrecise` to a locked-down `Math`. Bindery now loads the library with a temporary stand-in in that situation (and logs a warning in the console), so the import works; nothing changes when `Math` is intact.

## [0.1.0]

### Added

- **PDF image import:** images can be imported into Foundry VTT as scene backgrounds with grid alignment, illustrations in journal entries, and files ready for use as tokens.
- **Review screen (ApplicationV2):** an image list, a page preview with bounding boxes around detected regions, a diagnostics panel, and destination selection for each image: Scene / Journal handout / Token / Unassigned. Destinations can also be assigned in bulk. Nothing is saved without user approval.
- **Manual region extraction ("Select & cut"):** selecting a rectangular area on a PDF page lets you extract it as a new image.
- **Journal entry grouping:** images assigned to the "Journal" destination with a shared group name are combined into a single `JournalEntry`, with one page per image. This allows you, for example, to create one journal entry containing materials for a book chapter instead of a separate entry for every image.
- **Image naming:** each image's name can be edited directly in the review list.
- **Rotation and cropping:** images can be rotated, changing the orientation of the illustration itself rather than just the crop rectangle. Resize handles let you adjust the boundaries of automatically detected regions.
- **Token preparation panel:** selecting the "Token" destination enables cropping and zooming within one of four mask shapes: circle, square, rounded square, or hexagon. The panel also supports background removal with adjustable tolerance, manual touch-ups, applying a built-in ring frame or uploading a custom one, and image sharpening. Background removal starts at the corners; enclosed areas the algorithm cannot reach can be removed with a manual click. All changes are previewed live over a checkerboard before saving. The `images.removeTokenBackgroundDefault` profile setting lets you enable background removal by default for a given book.
- **Legal notice and protected file handling:** added a one-time acknowledgment of the legal notice. The module does not bypass passwords or DRM protection. Attempting to use a protected PDF displays a clear error message and stops processing.
- **Interface languages:** English and Polish.
